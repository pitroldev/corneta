## The matrix that matters

Values reviewed on October 18, 2026 for H.264 video. They are a starting point, not an order to fill all the bandwidth you have.

| Output  |     Twitch |                         YouTube |             Kick |
| ------- | ---------: | ------------------------------: | ---------------: |
| 720p30  | 3,000 kbps |  8,000 recommended (min. 3,000) | 2,500–4,500 kbps |
| 720p60  | 4,500 kbps |  8,000 recommended (min. 3,000) | 3,500–5,000 kbps |
| 1080p30 | 4,500 kbps | 14,000 recommended (min. 5,000) | 4,500–6,000 kbps |
| 1080p60 | 6,000 kbps | 17,000 recommended (min. 6,000) | 6,000–8,000 kbps |

All three accept CBR and a keyframe every 2s. Kick caps H.264 at 8,000 kbps, 1080p and 60 FPS. Kick's per-resolution ranges come from the guide for the KICK - Go Live app; in its OBS guide, it only sets 1,000 to 8,000 kbps. YouTube accepts more bitrate and creates smaller versions for viewers; in AV1 or HEVC, its recommended values are lower. On Twitch, 6,000 kbps is still the official reference for 1080p60.

## One signal, or one per platform?

If OBS sends 1080p60 at 6,000 kbps, **Straight up** is the lightest path: all three get the same signal. YouTube sits well below its recommended 17 Mbps, but still within the minimum it asks for 1080p60 in H.264.

If you want more quality on YouTube without going over the limits of Twitch and Kick, use **All out** and send a stronger signal from OBS: Corneta transcodes each platform to its own preset. YouTube goes out at 9,000 kbps by default, and you can raise it in fine-tuning. **Smart** doesn't do this on its own: it copies OBS to Twitch, YouTube and Kick and only transcodes destinations that need vertical video, like TikTok and Instagram. Each mode shows the total upload and the load on your PC; if the transcodes go beyond what your graphics card can handle, the screen warns you.

## Do the math before you hit GO LIVE

Add up video and audio for every platform that is turned on: copied or transcoded, each output goes up separately. Then compare the total with a test run while the scene has motion. As a practical margin, YouTube itself recommends an upload between 1.5× and 2× the combined bitrate when simulcasting.

<Callout label="More bitrate won't fix a heavy scene" tone="limit">
  If OBS is losing frames to rendering or encoding, raising the bitrate only
  adds pressure on the network. First find out which counter got worse.
</Callout>

To pick a mode without memorizing this table, see [Straight up, Smart or All out](/en/help/quality/quality-modes).
