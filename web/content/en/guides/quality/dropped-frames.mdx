## Open the right counter

In OBS, open **View → Stats** during the same scene that usually lags. Don't draw conclusions from the CPU or the game's FPS alone.

| What went up                                   | Likely bottleneck                                        | First test                                     |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Dropped Frames (Network)                       | with Corneta, a local bottleneck between OBS and Corneta | close heavy programs and cut transcodes        |
| Frames missed due to rendering lag             | GPU without time to build the scene                      | cap the game's FPS                             |
| Skipped frames due to encoding lag             | encoder not delivering in time                           | lower resolution and FPS; then a faster preset |
| Drops or reconnects on one platform in Corneta | upload, Wi-Fi, route or ingest                           | cable, no VPN and a lower bitrate              |

## Network

When OBS sends straight to the platform, **Dropped Frames (Network)** measures your internet. With Corneta, OBS hands off the video inside your own PC (`127.0.0.1`), so that counter points to a local bottleneck, not to your internet.

Internet problems show up in Corneta, platform by platform, as reconnects and drops. That's when it pays to close backup and sync, test over a cable, turn off your VPN and lower the bitrate. If only one platform reconnects, look into that platform before you touch OBS as a whole. Don't change the OBS server: it has to keep pointing at Corneta.

## Rendering

OBS uses the GPU to build the scene even when the video is encoded on the CPU. A game with uncapped FPS, heavy Browser Sources and filters can take up the time that ran short. Cap the game, run OBS as administrator and turn off one source at a time.

## Encoding

Here the frame was built, but the encoder fell behind. Lower resolution and FPS; if that isn't enough, use a faster preset. Your graphics card's hardware encoder usually takes load off the CPU, and going from 60 to 30 FPS cuts the number of frames to encode in half. In multistream, cutting transcodes counts too.
