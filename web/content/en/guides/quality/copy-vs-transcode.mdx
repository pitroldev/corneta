## Copying keeps what came in

When copying, Corneta forwards the video it receives without recreating its frames. That cuts CPU and GPU work, but it doesn't change resolution, FPS, video bitrate or framing.

Audio can be converted to the output format, or processed by the normalizer when it is turned on. The video mode alone doesn't decide whether the audio gets transcoded.

## Transcoding creates a new output

Transcoding decodes the video and produces a separate live output. It's the way to go when:

- a platform accepts less bitrate;
- a destination needs a different resolution or FPS;
- a vertical stream requires a 9:16 crop;
- the incoming codec doesn't work on the platform.

## The decision in three questions

1. Do all of them accept exactly the signal that came in? Use **Straight up**.
2. Do only one or two need adjusting? Use **Smart**.
3. Do you want an independent preset per platform, and can your machine handle it? Use **All out**.

Don't pick All out just because the name sounds better. If three outputs already accept the same 1080p60, transcoding three times can burn GPU without any visible difference.

<Callout label="Copying won't fix a bad signal" tone="limit">
  If your streaming software already sends a blurry picture, clipping audio or a
  wrong keyframe setting, Straight up replicates the problem. Fix the source, or
  transcode with a clear goal.
</Callout>

See the names and the estimate in Help: [which quality mode to choose](/en/help/quality/quality-modes).
