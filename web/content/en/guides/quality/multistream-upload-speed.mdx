## The math that matters

Add up the **video and audio** bitrate of each local output. Then leave a margin
for Wi-Fi, other people on the network and swings from your provider. YouTube
recommends aiming for 1.5 to 2 times the combined bitrate when simulstreaming.

Corneta's **Quality** screen already says **fits your upload with room to
spare** at 20% headroom. On Wi-Fi or a shared network, aim for the 1.5× to 2×
range.

If Twitch uses 6 Mb/s and YouTube uses 4 Mb/s, the total is 10 Mb/s. The stable
upload to aim for sits between 15 and 20 Mb/s. That is not a promise from your
provider: it helps you decide whether this setup is worth testing.

## What goes into the total

Every destination that leaves your PC directly counts. If three platforms get
6 Mb/s each, you are not using 6 Mb/s: you are trying to sustain close to
18 Mb/s, plus audio, protocol overhead and fluctuations.

A cloud relay changes the local math. Your PC sends one output to the relay, and
the relay copies it out on its side. You use less upload at home, but you now
depend on that service and the limits of its plan.

## Your plan's Mbps is not stable Mbps

The number on your plan is a ceiling, not a guarantee of stability every
second. Run the test on the same network, at the same time of day and with the
cable or Wi-Fi you use when you stream.

OBS explains that dropped frames show up when the connection to the server
can't keep up with the bitrate you set. A quick test can show a high number and
still hide packet loss, jitter or short drops.

## Test with your real stream

1. Close backups, sync and downloads on other devices.
2. Open your game, camera, microphone, alerts and your heaviest scene.
3. On the **Live** screen, use **Upload bandwidth → Measure now** while you are
   not streaming.
4. Do an unlisted or test stream with every destination.
5. Watch bitrate and dropped frames for a few minutes, especially in scenes
   with motion.

## If the math doesn't add up

Lower the bitrate of the least demanding destination first. Then look at FPS
and resolution. Switching from **All out** to **Smart** takes load off your PC:
Twitch, YouTube and Kick get a copy of the OBS signal, and only destinations
that need vertical video are transcoded. Your upload changes too, because each
copy goes up at the OBS bitrate. Check the number on the mode's card before you
decide.

If your connection can only hold one good output, use a cloud relay. Fighting
an upload that is already at its limit usually ends in dropped frames at the
worst moment.

<Callout label="Don't use download in the math" tone="warning">
  Your stream leaves from your PC. The number that matters is stable upload, not
  download or the highest peak you saw in the test.
</Callout>
