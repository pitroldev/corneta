## The path a stream takes

OBS **encodes** audio and video, sends them over **RTMP or RTMPS** to an **ingest** server, and the platform distributes versions to your viewers. In local multistream, Corneta receives one signal and opens that path for each destination.

## Connection terms

### RTMP

A widely used protocol for carrying audio, video and data from the encoder to the platform. It describes the transport; it is not the picture quality.

### RTMPS

RTMP protected by TLS. It encrypts the leg between your encoder and the server. Always prefer it when the platform offers it.

### Ingest

The server that receives your stream. The **ingest URL** gives the address; the **stream key** says which channel should receive the signal.

### Stream key

The secret that authorizes the broadcast. Treat it like a password: don't show it in screenshots, videos, logs or diagnostics.

## Picture terms

### Resolution

The number of pixels in the frame, such as 1280×720 or 1920×1080. More pixels only turn into more detail when bitrate and encoder keep up.

### FPS

Frames per second. 60 FPS makes motion smoother, but doubles the volume of frames compared with 30 FPS.

### Bitrate

The amount of data sent per second. In live streaming, it usually shows up in kbps or Mbps. Adding up output bitrates helps estimate upload; on its own, it doesn't measure the final quality.

### Keyframe

A complete frame used as a reference point. Between keyframes, the encoder describes changes. Twitch, YouTube and Kick usually work with a 2-second interval.

### CBR

Rate control that tries to keep the bitrate constant. It's the default recommended by the major platforms for live ingest.

## Processing terms

### Encoder

Turns frames and audio into a compressed stream. It can use the CPU, like x264, or dedicated hardware, like NVENC, QSV and AMF.

### Codec

The compression standard the encoder uses, such as H.264, HEVC or AV1. The encoder does the work; the codec is the format of that work.

### Copy or passthrough

Copies the already encoded stream without recreating the video. Saves CPU and GPU, but keeps the resolution, FPS, bitrate and orientation it received.

### Transcode

Decodes and encodes another version. Lets you change resolution, FPS, bitrate or framing, but costs processing power and can lose quality.

### Relay

An intermediary that receives a stream and forwards it somewhere else. It can be local, like Corneta, or in the cloud, like restream services.

## Diagnostic terms

### Dropped frames

Frames that didn't reach the destination because of a network problem. In OBS, don't confuse them with frames skipped because of rendering or encoding.

### Render lag

OBS couldn't build the frames in time, usually because of a lack of GPU resources or a heavy scene.

### Encoding lag

The encoder didn't finish the frames in time. It can point to CPU, GPU, preset or too many sessions.

When a counter gets worse, use the [dropped frames, rendering and encoding](/en/guides/quality/dropped-frames) decision tree before you change everything at once.
