## Build two outputs, not two improvised streams

Use OBS in 16:9 as the main source. In Corneta, leave Quality on **Smart**: it copies Twitch, Kick and YouTube when the signal already fits and re-encodes the upright 9:16 destination on its own. Just don't force **Copy** on that destination or use **Straight up**: then the video goes out sideways and either comes out wrong or doesn't get in at all.

<ContentImage baseName="corneta-vertical-target-settings-en" />

The vertical destination uses an encoder session and more upload. The format doesn't come from rotating the picture: it comes from choosing what stays inside the narrow frame.

On YouTube, the platform itself creates a 9:16 version of your horizontal stream by default, with a center crop or other layouts, when you stream through Live Control Room. Decide before going live: YouTube doesn't let you add the vertical version after the stream has started.

## Choose the framing

1. Add TikTok or Instagram in **Platforms**. They're Corneta's upright destinations, still marked as experimental.
2. Enter the URL and key provided by the platform's dashboard.
3. Open **Frame the 9:16** and click **Grab a frame from OBS** to see your real scene. Grabbing a frame works while OBS is live; if you don't move anything, the center crop is used.
4. Position the 9:16 strip over your face, camera, scoreboard or the main action.
5. Adjust the zoom without cutting off essential text.
6. Save and check the result with real movement.

<ContentImage baseName="corneta-vertical-reframe-editor-en" />

The editor is manual. Corneta doesn't promise auto-reframing and doesn't follow a person on its own. If the focus moves around a lot during the stream, set up a layout that stays readable in the center or use a tool with an independent vertical canvas.

## What fits in the center

A 9:16 crop of a 16:9 frame throws away a good part of the sides. Before you stream:

- move your webcam and important captions to the center zone;
- avoid scoreboards pinned to the corners;
- don't use small text designed only for horizontal 1080p;
- test the game's menus, map and HUD;
- check the overlay on a phone, not just on your monitor.

## What it costs your machine

A vertical output has to scale and re-encode. If OBS already uses the GPU encoder, confirm how many simultaneous sessions your driver supports. Corneta sends the vertical output at 720×1280 and 30 FPS. What you can raise is the bitrate, from **Standard** to **Sharp** in the fine-tuning on the **Quality** screen, and only after a stable test stream.

<Callout label="Twitch Dual Format is a different flow" tone="note">
  Twitch has rolled out Dual Format to all streamers. On OBS 32 or newer, it
  uses Enhanced Broadcasting with a vertical canvas, such as the one from the
  Aitum Vertical plugin. It's a separate flow between OBS and Twitch. Don't
  confuse this feature with sending an independent vertical RTMP output to
  another platform.
</Callout>
