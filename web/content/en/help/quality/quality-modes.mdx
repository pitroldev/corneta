## The short answer

- **Straight up:** when OBS already delivers a resolution, FPS and bitrate that
  every platform accepts.
- **Smart:** when the landscape platforms accept the OBS signal and only the
  vertical ones, like TikTok and Instagram, need a different format.
- **All out:** when each platform needs to get its own preset and your GPU or
  CPU can handle one re-encode per destination.

## Straight up

Corneta copies the compressed video from OBS to every destination. It doesn't
open a new video re-encode per platform, so it's the lightest mode.

The limit shows up early: every platform gets the same video. If the setup that
works well for YouTube goes over what another destination accepts, set OBS to
the lowest limit or switch modes. A vertical platform, like TikTok or Instagram,
comes out sideways on a copy.

## Smart

Corneta copies OBS to the landscape platforms, like Twitch, YouTube and Kick,
and re-encodes only the vertical ones on its own. It doesn't compare the OBS
signal with each platform's limit: on the copies, the OBS bitrate is what goes
out. The **Quality** screen shows the maximum the copied platforms accept.

Only Smart lets you choose, in **Fine-tuning per platform**, between **Auto**,
**Copy** and **Convert** for each destination.

Check the mode cards: they show the total **Upload** and the estimated **Load on
your PC**. If the re-encodes go beyond what the graphics card can handle, the
screen warns you.

## All out

Each destination gets its own re-encode, using the platform's preset. In
**Fine-tuning per platform**, you choose the picture quality (**Light**,
**Standard** or **Sharp**), the bitrate and **What converts it**: graphics card
or processor. Resolution and FPS follow each platform's preset. It's the mode
that puts the most load on the CPU or GPU. On cards with a limit on simultaneous
sessions, the math may not add up.

## How to decide on your PC

1. Start on **Straight up** if every platform accepts the OBS output.
2. Switch to **Smart** when you have a vertical platform or when only one
   destination needs to change. In that case, choose **Convert** on that one
   only.
3. Use **All out** when per-platform tuning is worth the extra load.
4. Do a test stream with your real game, camera, alerts and motion.
5. On the **Live** screen, watch CPU and GPU in the **Your machine** panel and
   each platform's **FPS** and **Drops** during the heaviest stretch.

| Symptom                       | Quick test                            | Next change                              |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Encoder overloaded            | Switch from All out to Smart          | Reduce the number of re-encodes          |
| GPU maxed out with the game   | Cap the game's FPS                    | Leave GPU time for OBS and the encoder   |
| Upload at the limit           | Check the **Upload** on the mode card | Lower the bitrate or use a cloud relay   |
| A platform rejects the signal | Compare its preset                    | Re-encode only that destination in Smart |

<Callout label="The estimate doesn't replace a test stream" tone="limit">
  An available encoder doesn't mean a stable game. Test the heaviest scene and
  keep the **Live** screen open before you bring your audience in.
</Callout>
